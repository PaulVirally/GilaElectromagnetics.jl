\subsection{Which offsets Gila evaluates, and its use of reflections}
\label{sec:grd:cnt}

Gila evaluates $N^3$ relative offsets --- \code{egoToe}, indexed $i \leftrightarrow i-1$
cells, all components non-negative --- and fills the doubled $(2N)^3$ circulant grid from
them by reflection with sign flips (\code{egoToeCrc!}, whose rule is
$M[(c,a),(c',b)] = \code{egoToe}[a,b,|d|+1]\,\sigma_a\sigma_b$ with
$\sigma_m = \operatorname{sign}(d_m)$). \emph{The factor of eight at stake in the task
statement is therefore already taken}; the count at $128^3$ is $2\,097\,144$ separated
offsets, not $1.7\times10^{7}$. The eight indices with all components $\le 2$ go through
the contact path (\code{egoFunSng!}, \code{wekTrp}), and \code{egoSrfFxd!} returns
\code{Inf} on them, so the far method's domain is exactly \code{isFarInd}, $\max_i i_i \ge
3$. The reflection rule was checked end to end: the circulant arrays built from Gila's own
fill and from \code{farBlock!} agree to $9.81\times10^{-15}$ relative at $f = 1$ and
$8.68\times10^{-15}$ at $f = 1+0.1\ii$ over a $20^3$ grid, with zero sign-flipped entries
out of $8748$.

Four things this fixes.

\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{The face pairs themselves are good and the assembly is what is lost.} Per-pair
errors are $10^{-15}$ to $9\times10^{-14}$ everywhere; the assembled tensor is $10$ to
$1000$ times worse. The amplification grows as $(|\mathbf{R}|/s)^2$ on an axis offset --- it
is a \emph{fine}-cell effect, worst at $\lambda/32$ and mildest at $\lambda/4$ --- and is
flat at $312$ on the body diagonal.
\item \textbf{The slender cell's long axes lose four to five digits to the assembly alone.}
Face pairs good to $2.6\times10^{-15}$ become a tensor good to $1.1\times10^{-11}$ at
$64$ cells, because the amplification reaches $1.67\times10^{5}$.
\item \textbf{Along the slender cell's short axis Gila's fixed rule is wrong by $O(1)$}:
relative tensor errors of $0.789$, $0.227$, $3.68\times10^{-2}$, $4.50\times10^{-3}$,
$1.79\times10^{-3}$, $2.97\times10^{-6}$, $8.94\times10^{-7}$, $9.12\times10^{-9}$ at
$n = 2,3,4,6,8,16,32,64$, and per entry up to $1.60$. \code{quadOrd} keys the rule order on
the separation in \emph{cells}, so a $1/512$ boundary layer across a $1/32$ face is
invisible to it. (The rows at $n = 3, 6, 32, 64$ use the graded volume reference of
\code{refquad.jl}: \code{pairKer} did not return on those panels in $25$ minutes.)
\item \textbf{The target the expansion has to beat is therefore $10^{-13}$ per entry on the
cubes and something much better than $10^{-11}$ on the slender cell.} The claim in the task
statement that the far tensor is good to about $10^{-10}$ is pessimistic for cubes ---
$5\times10^{-14}$ to $8.7\times10^{-13}$ is what it is --- and optimistic for the slender
cell.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The volume identity in exact arithmetic}
\label{sec:grd:div}

Part (a), table \code{a\_div.txt}. On the polynomial kernels $F = |\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}|^{2j}$
both sides of the divergence identity are rational numbers in the edge lengths, so they can
be compared with no arithmetic error at all. The thirty-six face-pair moments, from an exact
multinomial integrator on the \code{facePair} panels and summed with the \code{srfSum!}
signs, against the difference-box form with the exact weight moments, at
$\mathbf{n} \in \{(2,0,0),(3,1,0),(2,2,2)\}$, $j = 1,2,3$, for the cubic and the slender
cell: \textbf{$\max|\text{difference}| = 0/1$ exactly in all eighteen rows}. The theory is
Section~\ref{sec:vol}; the smallest case can be checked by hand and is written out there.
The weight moments themselves were checked separately (\code{a\_mu.txt}): the library's
closed form against the same integral done as two elementary halves, both in
\code{Rational\{BigInt\}}, \textbf{zero disagreements} over five edge lengths and
$n = 0,\dots,24$.

\subsection{The conditioning of the derivative recursions}
\label{sec:grd:cond}

The Cartesian organization of the expansion needs $\partial^{\boldsymbol\alpha}g(\mathbf{R})$
to total order $25$--$40$, and whether that is stable in \code{Float64} decided the shape of
the whole design. Three independent measurements, all against $220$- or $256$-bit
references, all reporting
$\log_{10}\bigl(|x_{64}-x_{\mathrm{ref}}|/(\varepsilon|x_{\mathrm{ref}}|)\bigr)$.

\paragraph{Hobson's formula is unusable.} Building the radial derivatives $g^{(n)}$ from
$rg' = (\ii kr - 1)g$, then $T_m = (r^{-1}\dd/\dd r)^m g$, then the Cartesian derivatives
from the $\nu$-sum, loses $7.3$ to $8.4$ digits by $|\boldsymbol\alpha| = 25$ at
$k\mathbf{R} \le 3$, and $10.4$ digits at $|\boldsymbol\alpha| = 30$,
$k\mathbf{R} = 10$. The loss is the alternating cancellation inside the $\nu$-sum, where
$\mathbf{R}^{\boldsymbol\alpha-2\boldsymbol\nu}T_{p-|\boldsymbol\nu|}$ terms of comparable
size subtract. \emph{Discarded.}

\paragraph{The coupled Cartesian recurrence is fine.} With $u = g$, $w = rg$, the identities
$r^2\partial_j u + x_j u - \ii k x_j w = 0$ and $\partial_j w = \ii k x_j u$, differentiated
$\boldsymbol\alpha$ times and solved for the highest index with the pivot
$j = \arg\max_i|R_i|$, lose $1.4$ digits at $p = 25$, $k\mathbf{R} = 1$ on the axis and less
off it ($1.17$ face diagonal, $0.85$ body diagonal, $0.38$ generic). The worst band is
$k\mathbf{R} \approx 10$, where the loss rises to $4.6$ digits at $p = 25$; below
$k\mathbf{R} = 3$ it is flat at $1.5$--$2$ and above $k\mathbf{R} = 30$ it falls back to
about one, because the exponential then dominates and nothing cancels. \emph{Answer to the
design question: yes, the \code{Float64} recursion is stable to order $25$ at
$k\mathbf{R} \approx 1$, provided it is this route.}

\paragraph{The pair recurrence on $(G,S)$ is better still, and it is the one that was
audited.} With $G = g(\mathbf{R}+\boldsymbol\delta)$ and
$S = |\mathbf{R}+\boldsymbol\delta|\,G$, the two systems
$\nabla S = \ii k(\mathbf{R}+\boldsymbol\delta)G$ and
$P\nabla G = (\mathbf{R}+\boldsymbol\delta)(\ii kS - G)$, $P = |\mathbf{R}+\boldsymbol\delta|^2$,
give a two-level recurrence whose \code{Float64} digits lost, maximised over four directions
and two frequencies, are

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrrrrr@{}}
\toprule
$k\mathbf{R}$ & 0.3 & 1.0 & 3.0 & 10 & 30 & 100 & 300\\
\midrule
order 10 & 1.06 & 0.87 & 0.93 & 1.01 & 1.13 & 1.66 & 2.15\\
order 20 & 1.25 & 1.11 & 1.13 & 2.07 & 1.13 & 1.67 & 2.15\\
order 30 & 1.39 & 1.31 & 1.37 & 2.04 & 1.84 & 1.68 & 2.15\\
order 40 & 1.41 & 1.39 & 1.54 & 2.02 & 3.84 & 1.70 & 2.15\\
\bottomrule

\noindent
The flat floors at $k\mathbf{R} = 100$ and $300$ are the \emph{seed} $e^{\ii k\varrho}$,
which carries $k\varrho\,\varepsilon$ of relative error and never degrades with order; a
double-double phase (twelve flops) removes it, recovering $2.5$ of the $3.35$ digits lost at
$k\mathbf{R} = 3000$, and is used for the $h_0$ seed of the shipped method, where
$k\mathbf{R}$ reaches about $350$ on a $128^3$ block at $\lambda/4$. The independent audit of
this route put it at $\le 2.15$ digits through order $30$ at every $k\mathbf{R}$ and
$\le 1.4$ digits for $k\mathbf{R} \le 3$, cross-checked against four other routes to
$10^{-65}$ at $220$ bits.

\paragraph{And the quantity that matters loses nothing.} The individual degree sums
$S_p = \sum_{|\boldsymbol\alpha|=p}\partial^{\boldsymbol\alpha}g\,
\mu_{\boldsymbol\alpha}/\boldsymbol\alpha!$ lose $3.2$ to $6.3$ digits relative to
\emph{themselves}, but $|S_{30}|/|S_0|$ is $10^{-9}$ to $10^{-29}$ and
$\sum_p|S_p|/|\sum_p S_p|$ is $1.00$ to $1.74$: over the whole physical grid the accumulated
tensor loses between $-1.06$ and $+0.68$ digits. \emph{The moment weights kill exactly the
orders at which the recursion loses digits}, and the a priori amplification $1/(1-\rho) =
7.7$ at $\rho = 0.87$ is itself four to seven times pessimistic. This is why no
extended-precision path is needed in the per-offset evaluation --- and why the one place
that does need exact arithmetic is the geometry table, whose contraction cancels by
$7.3\times10^{11}$ (Section~\ref{sec:bnd:notproven}).

\subsection{The exact moments}
\label{sec:grd:mom}

The only geometry the far field ever needs is the moment of the triangle weight,
\begin{equation}
\mu_n(s) = \int_{-s}^{s}(s-|t|)\,t^n\,\dd t =
\begin{cases}\dfrac{2s^{n+2}}{(n+1)(n+2)}, & n\ \text{even},\\[4pt] 0, & n\ \text{odd},\end{cases}
\qquad
\int_D w\,\boldsymbol{\delta}^{\boldsymbol\alpha}\dd\boldsymbol\delta
= \prod_{i=1}^{3}\mu_{\alpha_i}(s_i),
\label{eq:grd:mu}
\end{equation}
an exact rational in the edge lengths, zero unless every $\alpha_i$ is even. Note
$\mu_0(s) = s^2$, so $\int_D w = V_{\mathrm{t}}^2$ and the cell average
$(1/V_{\mathrm{t}})\int_D w$ is $V_{\mathrm{t}}$. Sample values, all verified as exact
rationals (\code{a\_mu.txt}): at $s = 1/32$, $\mu_0 = 1/1024$, $\mu_2 = 1/6291456$,
$\mu_4 = 1/16106127360$, $\mu_{12} = 1/107433837485284428611584$. The corresponding volume
moments $\int_D w|\mathbf{R}+\boldsymbol\delta|^{2j}$ were computed by two independent exact
routes --- the multinomial expansion against \eqref{eq:grd:mu} and the face-pair route ---
and agree exactly; at $\mathbf{s} = (1/32)^3$,

\subsection{The joint error target}
\label{sec:grd:tgt}

Three numbers set what the kernel must deliver so that nothing downstream is limited by it.

\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{The FFT of the Toeplitz block.} At $N = 256$ on the doubled grid the round-off
of the forward transform is $5.3\times10^{-15}$ in \code{Float64} and $2.9\times10^{-6}$ in
\code{Float32}.
\item \textbf{The anti-Hermitian positivity check} of Section~2.4 of \emph{The Integrals of
Gila}. On a $6^3$ block at real frequency, where $\operatorname{Im}M$ must be positive
semi-definite, the measured floor is $\lambda_{\min} = -5\times10^{-15}$ against
$\lambda_{\max} = 0.077$, i.e.\ $6.5\times10^{-14}$ relative. \emph{A $10^{-13}$ kernel
error sits $1.5$ times above that floor}, which is the reason this task targets
$10^{-13}$ where the earlier work targeted $10^{-10}$.
\item \textbf{The solver does not need any of it.} Gila's default storage type is
\code{Float32} (\code{dfltPrc}) and the default relative tolerance of GMRES, BiCGStab and
the mixed-precision refinement is $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$: $1.49\times10^{-8}$ in
\code{Float64} and $3.45\times10^{-4}$ in \code{Float32}. \emph{The solve discards six to
eight digits regardless.} The target is not for the solve; it is so that the operator is
the operator it claims to be, so that the positivity check means something, and so that a
future solve at higher precision is not blocked by the build.
\end{itemize}

Finally, the scale against which ``$10^{-13}$'' is measured matters, because far entries are
small. At $\lambda/32$, $f = 1$, the largest entry of the tensor on an axis offset is
$2.08\times10^{-2}$ at two cells, $5.77\times10^{-4}$ at eight, $9.44\times10^{-5}$ at
thirty-two and $4.76\times10^{-5}$ at $128$, against a self term of order one (the identity
$-1/f^2$). \textbf{Accuracy ``relative to the largest entry of the tensor'' is four orders
looser than per-entry accuracy for a far offset, and per-entry relative accuracy in
\code{Float64} is the honest target throughout this document.} Both are reported everywhere.
