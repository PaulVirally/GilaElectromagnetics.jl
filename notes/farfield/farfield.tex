\title{The Far Field of Gila:\\ A Cell-Size Expansion for Every Separated Pair}
\author{Claude (Anthropic, Claude Fable 5.1)\\[2pt]\small prepared for Paul Virally, GilaElectromagnetics}
\date{8 September 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Every entry of Gila's discretized vacuum Green operator at a separated relative offset is
today a signed sum of thirty-six four-dimensional Gauss--Legendre quadratures of the
Helmholtz kernel over pairs of cell faces. It is the bottleneck of the build --- a $128^3$
self-volume at $\lambda/32$ costs $1.94\times10^{10}$ kernel evaluations and $1058$~s of
single-threaded fill --- and it is not accurate: the assembly amplifies the per-pair error
by $36$ to $1300$ on a cubic cell and by up to $1.67\times10^{5}$ on the slender cell Gila
actually uses, and along that cell's short axis the fixed rule is wrong by a factor of
order one. This document replaces the quadrature. The thirty-six face pairs are shown to be
one integral of a smooth function against a non-negative product weight over the difference
box of the pair; that integral is expanded by the spherical addition theorem into a
frequency-independent geometry table, exact in the edge lengths, and a per-offset sum of
elementary functions. Two proved theorems bound the truncation of exactly the sum the code
performs; they choose the multipole order per offset, and where they cannot, the eight
octants of the difference box are used instead, and where neither converges --- one band of
sixty-seven offsets on the $16{:}16{:}1$ slender cell --- the moment series of the companion
document is. The result, \code{notes/farfield/farfield.jl}, agrees with a $220$-bit reference to
$8.6\times10^{-15}$ in max-norm and $9.3\times10^{-15}$ per entry over $427$ tensors at four
cell shapes, five frequencies and separations from two to $128$ cells, losing at most
$1.59$ decimal digits to \code{Float64} rounding; over sixty random cases spanning aspect
ratios $1$ to $1020$ the truncation is certified by the bound with no violation and the
rounding error is at most $16.4\,\varepsilon$ times the largest tensor entry. The far field
of a $128^3$ self-volume at $\lambda/32$ is built in $0.200$~s on twelve threads against
$200.76$~s, a factor of $1002$, and the whole \code{GlaVacOprMem} build falls from
$206.54$~s to $6.73$~s. What remains of that build is the contact and touching-shell
integrals, whose order-$9$ rule is shown here to be wrong by $117\,\%$ on the touching shell
of a slender cell and to be the sole cause of the indefiniteness of that operator's
anti-Hermitian part; replacing it by the exact moment series makes the operator positive
definite.

A final section extends the method to the unequal cells of Gila's composite volumes, where
a coarse region meets a fine one. The per-axis weight becomes a trapezoid, which is the
difference of two triangles, so the geometry table, the parity argument and the truncation
theorem carry over with new moments and nothing else ($132$ exact-rational identities, all
zero); the sub-box theorem needs no change at all. The twenty-seven-piece split the task
statement proposed for the near band was built, measured, and dropped: it does not converge
where it is needed once the scale ratio reaches $4$. The near band is served instead by the
gcd average of equal-cell tensors written as a box sum over the fine block the same library
already fills, whose cancellation was measured at $1.0$--$2.3$. Over $438$ $220$-bit
references at fourteen unequal pairs, three frequencies and separations from one to
thirty-two fine cells the worst error is $3.4\times10^{-15}$ in max-norm and
$4.7\times10^{-15}$ per entry with no certificate violation; the $1.4\times10^{6}$-offset
block of a $\lambda/2$ coarse region against a $\lambda/32$ fine one is filled in $5.7$~s
on twelve threads, against about $1.8$~h for Gila's two separated-pair rules, whose adaptive
class is accurate to $10^{-6}$ and whose fixed rule is wrong by $1.8\times10^{-4}$ on a rod
approached from the side.
\end{abstract}

\noindent
\code{farfield.jl} is standalone for routes (i) and (ii). Route (iii) --- the $k$-series
fallback of Section~\ref{sec:join:slender} --- lazily \code{include}s
\code{notes/moments/moments.jl} and errors with a clear message if it is absent; a build
whose \code{ksrcache/} is populated never touches it, and the three cubic shapes never
reach it at all.

\subsection{The API}
\label{sec:use:api}

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}L{0.44\textwidth}L{0.5\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
\code{farTensor(D, s, f; tol, fs, dir)} & the $3\times3$ tensor at one offset
\code{D::NTuple\{3,Int\}} of a cell of edges \code{s::NTuple\{3,<:Rational\}} at frequency
\code{f::Complex\{T\}}; errors when $\max|D| \le 1$\\
\code{farBlock!(egoToe, s, f; tol, fs, dir)} & fills \code{egoToe[:,:,i1,i2,i3]} for every
index with \code{max(i) >= 3} (offset \code{i .- 1}), leaving the eight contact indices
untouched; threaded, bitwise identical at $1$, $4$ and $12$ threads\\
\code{isFarInd(i)} & the predicate that partition uses; its complement is exactly Gila's
\code{egoFunSng!} set\\
\code{farShape(s; dir)} & builds or loads the exact geometry table for one shape\\
\code{farSetup(s, f; tol, scl, dir)} & contracts it against one frequency and builds the
threshold table; returns the \code{FrqSet} to pass as \code{fs}\\
\code{farRoute(fs, D; cst)} & the route, the order $L$ and the eight sub-box orders for one
offset\\
\code{farRouteStat(fs, dim)} & counts, the whole-box $L$ histogram, and the (ii) and (iii)
offset lists of a whole block\\
\midrule
\code{farSetupX(sT, sS, f; tol, nBlk, offs, dir)} & the set of one \emph{unequal} pair
(Section~\ref{sec:xs}): the trapezoid table keyed on the unordered pair, the thresholds of
Theorem~\ref{thm:xs:reg}, the $n$-cut certified at the nearest whole-box offset of
\code{offs}; errors unless the edges are integer multiples per axis (Gila's
\code{GlaExtInf} rule)\\
\code{farRouteX(fx, R)} & \code{(kind, L)} for one exact-rational offset \code{R} of the
target centre, the source at the origin: $1$ whole trapezoid box, $2$ gcd box sum, $3$
$k$-series on the thirty-six unequal panels; errors when the cells touch or overlap\\
\code{farTensorX(R, sT, sS, f; tol, fs, cert, dir)} & the $3\times3$ tensor of the pair
at one offset; \code{cert = true} also returns the certified bound on
$\max_{ab}|T_{ab}-G_{ab}|$\\
\code{farBlockX!(G, Rs, sT, sS, f; tol, fs, cert, dir, tms)} & fills
\code{G[:,:,q]} for every offset \code{Rs[q]::NTuple\{3,Rational\{BigInt\}\}}, threaded,
the near band as one box sum over a fine equal-cell block filled by \code{farBlock!};
returns the route kinds (and the certificates); \code{tms} receives the phase times\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
The library's own bounds and special functions --- \code{est}, \code{jbnd}, \code{hbnd},
\code{jtail}, \code{bndTrm}, \code{bndCum}, \code{boundL}, \code{boundLoct},
\code{costWhl}, \code{costOct}, \code{tnsWhl!}, \code{tnsOct!}, \code{tnsKsr},
\code{srfSum} --- are all reachable, and \code{verify.jl} uses every one of them. The
defaults are \code{TOL = 1e-14}, \code{LMAX = 56}, \code{NMAX = 12} (a floor, not a cap; see
Remark~\ref{rem:bnd:adapt}), \code{NCAP = 40}, \code{TABPRC = 192},
\code{BLDPRC = 256}, \code{KSRPRC = 128}. The two cache directories are
\code{Ref}s, \code{TABDIR[]} and \code{KSRDIR[]}, and every entry point also takes a
\code{dir} keyword, which is the safer way to point at a table directory: a missing table is
not an error, it is a silent rebuild.

The intended use inside a build is one \code{farSetup} per (shape, frequency) followed by
one \code{farBlock!}:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{minipage}{0.86\textwidth}
\begin{verbatim}
include("notes/farfield/farfield.jl")
s = (1//32, 1//32, 1//32); f = 1.0 + 0.0im
fs = farSetup(map(Rational{BigInt}, s), f; nBlk = 128)  # once per (shape,f)
farBlock!(egoToe, s, f; fs = fs)                       # every far offset
\end{verbatim}
\end{minipage}
\end{center}

\noindent
and, for the external block between a fine and a coarse region, one \code{farSetupX}
per (pair, frequency) followed by one \code{farBlockX!} over the block's non-touching
offsets (\code{Rs} as exact rationals, target centre minus source centre):

\begin{center}\small
\begin{minipage}{0.86\textwidth}
\begin{verbatim}
sT = (1//32, 1//32, 1//32)                  # the fine target cell
sS = (1//2, 1//2, 1//2)                     # the coarse source cell
Q = Rational{BigInt}
fx = farSetupX(map(Q, sT), map(Q, sS), f; offs = Rs)  # once per (pair,f)
farBlockX!(G, Rs, sT, sS, f; fs = fx)          # G is 3 x 3 x length(Rs)
\end{verbatim}
\end{minipage}
\end{center}

\subsection{What it costs the first time}
\label{sec:use:cost}

The exact geometry table is built once per cell shape and is frequency independent; every
frequency after the first costs a contraction of tens of milliseconds. The table is
$16$--$20$~MB on disk (the four shipped in \code{shapetab/} are $19.5$~MB each) and reloads
in one to two seconds. Cold, single-threaded, one shape per
process, sized for a $32^3$ block:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrrr@{}}
\toprule
shape & $L_{\mathrm{whl}}$ & $L_{\mathrm{oct}}$ & $n_{\max}$ & build $+$ setup & peak RSS\\
\midrule
$(1/32)^3$ & 48 & 51 & 12 & $14.3$~s & $0.66$~GB\\
$(1/8)^3$  & 50 & 51 & 12 & $14.8$~s & $0.67$~GB\\
$(1/4)^3$  & 52 & 52 & 12 & $15.7$~s & $0.70$~GB\\
$(1/32,1/32,1/512)$ & 56 & 55 & 12 & $18.3$~s & $0.74$~GB\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
The build allocates nothing per term. Its three inner loops --- the integer
solid-harmonic recurrence \eqref{eq:exp:rec}, the multiplication by $|\boldsymbol{\delta}|^2$
and the moment accumulator --- run in preallocated GMP and MPFR registers through the
in-place \code{Base.GMP.MPZ} and \code{mpfr\_*} calls, one distinct \code{BigInt} per
polynomial coefficient. The allocating first version of the same loops peaked at $21$ to
$25$~GB of resident memory and took $44.5$ to $64.7$~s per shape: $2.4$ to $5.8$~MB of
\code{BigInt} garbage per $(l,m)$ column, $15$~GB over the $2601$ columns of an $L = 50$
octant table, which neither a forced collection every sixteen columns nor a heap-size hint
returned to the system. The in-place rewrite was checked bit-identical against it on
$24$ whole-box and octant tables (three shapes, $n_{\max} \in \{6, 12\}$, $L \in \{16, 20\}$,
segments from $l = 0$ and from $l > 0$), and the cold cache files above are byte-for-byte the
sizes the allocating version wrote. The peak of $0.66$ to $0.74$~GB is now the Julia
process itself.

A table already on disk removes even this: setup is then $0.7$ to $1.3$~s, and the
per-offset cost of the fill is $665$ to $1470$~ns single-threaded (Section~\ref{sec:bch}).
The one remaining cold cost is the $k$-series fallback, which needs
\code{notes/moments/moments.jl}: on the slender needle its $67$ offsets take $128$~s on
eight threads from cold ($633$~s serially), against $0.14$~s for the other $524\,213$, and
are then cached to \code{ksrcache/} per (shape, frequency).

The table of an unequal pair costs what the equal-cell whole box costs at the same $L$:
$3.3$--$5.7$~s cold per pair for $L = 40$--$56$ against $4.0$~s for the equal-cell whole
box to $L = 56$ under the same load, $0.2$--$0.3$~GB over the loaded process, and
$0.4$--$2.5$~MB on disk, because a pair has no octant table
(Section~\ref{sec:xs:ver}). The near band of such a block also needs the equal-cell set of
the gcd cell, which is the shipped table when the fine cell is one of the four shapes.
