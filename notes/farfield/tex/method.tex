\section{Acknowledgement of method}
\label{sec:meth}

This document, the library it describes and every measurement in it were produced by Claude
(Anthropic, Claude Fable~5.1) over two working days in September 2026, run as a portfolio of
independent sub-agents coordinated by a root agent, in the same way as the two companion
documents.

The first round launched eight sub-agents on the seven approach families of the task
statement plus the groundwork, with no agent told which family looked promising and no agent
reading another's transcript --- only its report. That independence was the point: three of
the families converged on the same object from different directions (the Cartesian Taylor
series, the derivative-free coefficient recurrence and the addition theorem are one
expansion in three organizations), and the fact that they agreed to $10^{-14}$ per entry
over $16\,340$ offsets, having been written without knowledge of each other, is a stronger
statement than any one of them verifying itself. Two families were killed by measurement in
that round --- the spectral route by the non-decaying periodization it implies
($768$~GB at the frequency Gila uses), the interpolation route by an arithmetic cost above
the direct method's --- and both are recorded in Section~\ref{sec:reg} with the number that
killed them, so that they are not reopened by intuition.

The second round was a chain rather than a portfolio: a theory pass that wrote and checked
every bound, a unification pass that built the library, a pass that sharpened the bound by a
median factor of $3.2\times10^{3}$, a preparation pass that integrated it, an
\emph{adversarial} audit whose only instruction was to break the library, and a merge pass
that fixed what the audit found. The audit is the reason this document can distinguish what
is certified from what is measured. It found ten defects, one of which
(Section~\ref{sec:def}, D9) destroyed the answer completely outside the tested range while
the library's own bound reported success, and it found it by refusing to stay inside the
range the task statement named. Two conclusions this work had already written down twice ---
that the slender operator's indefiniteness is a property of the discretization, and that the
$\lambda/128$ cube's error was rounding --- were both wrong, and both were overturned by
later passes with a measurement.

Paul Virally ran the independent Mathematica cross-check of the volume formulation himself
(Section~\ref{sec:vol:pin}): an \code{NIntegrate} of
$(1/V_{\mathrm{t}})\int_D w\,[(\partial_a\partial_b + \delta_{ab}k^2)g](\mathbf{R}+
\boldsymbol\delta)\,\dd\boldsymbol\delta$ at $\mathbf{n} = (5,1,0)$,
$\mathbf{s} = (1/32)^3$, $f = 1$, at working precision $30$--$40$ with the box split at the
kink of the weight, in his own installation, sharing no code with anything here. The
off-diagonal entry agreed with the $220$-bit face-pair reference to $3.4\times10^{-14}$,
which is Mathematica's own error estimate; the diagonal entry, recomputed with a fixed
$30$-point Gauss--Kronrod rule per sub-box after the global adaptive strategy stalled,
reproduced $0.001570738221266370299274 + 0.000360155079207178606379\,\ii$ in every printed
digit. That is the third of the three routes that pin the sign and the normalization, and it
is the only one with no author in common with the other two.

The machine was an Apple M3 Pro, 12 cores, 36~GB, Julia 1.12.6, with no CUDA device. A
hard limit of two concurrent Julia processes per agent was imposed after ten agents running
seventy-seven processes crashed the machine and lost two hours of work; the load average is
recorded next to every timing in the sub-agent reports, and every absolute timing quoted in
Section~\ref{sec:bch} was re-measured on a quiet machine at the end, because the round-1
timings were taken with four to ten other processes running and are only good for ordering
methods against each other.
