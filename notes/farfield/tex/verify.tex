\section{Verification}
\label{sec:ver}

Every number in this section comes from \code{notes/farfield/verify.jl}, single-threaded,
against $220$-bit references held in \code{refcache/reftensors.txt}. Three independent
references exist and were used: the \emph{face-pair} reference (\code{pairKer} of
\code{notes/gen/ref/mom.jl} on each of the thirty-six pairs, assembled with the
\code{srfSum!} signs), the \emph{volume} reference (a graded \code{BigFloat} tensor
Gauss--Legendre rule on the difference box, split at the kink of $w$ and graded to the
corner nearest the singularity), and a second volume rule written independently for family
(d). They agree with each other to $3.7\times10^{-65}$--$1.05\times10^{-49}$
(Section~\ref{sec:vol:pin}) and to $1.8\times10^{-61}$ on the cases where all three were
run. \code{pairKer} stalls beyond twenty-five minutes on slender panels, which is why the
slender short-axis rows use the volume rule.

\subsection{Against the reference: all bands, shapes, directions, frequencies}
\label{sec:ver:ref}

\code{verify.jl} part (d), table \code{d\_ref.txt}: every cached reference, $427$ rows.
$e_{\max} = \max_{ab}|G-G_{\mathrm{ref}}|/\max_{ab}|G_{\mathrm{ref}}|$; ``per entry'' is the
worst $|G_{ab}-G_{\mathrm{ref},ab}|/|G_{\mathrm{ref},ab}|$ over entries carrying more than
$10^{-8}$ of the largest; Re and Im are the same for the two parts separately, each relative
to that part's own value.

\begin{center}\footnotesize
\input{tex/tab/d_ref.tex}
\end{center}

\noindent
\textbf{Over $427$ tensors at four shapes, five frequencies, separations from two to $128$
cells and every offset direction, the worst max-norm error is $8.60\times10^{-15}$ and the
worst per-entry error is $9.34\times10^{-15}$.} The \emph{Re} and \emph{Im} columns above
$10^{-13}$ are the known formulation floor and nothing else: in every such row the offending
part carries less than about $1\,\%$ of that entry's own modulus, and its \emph{absolute}
error is still at $\varepsilon\max|G|$. Wherever both parts are $O(1)$ fractions of the
entry, both per-part errors are at $1.5\times10^{-14}$.

The bands are covered on both sides of every routing boundary. The boundaries bite only on
the two flat shapes, $(1/32,1/32,1/512)$ and $(1/8,1/8,1/128)$, and there both sides of all
five class boundaries --- $(0,0,n_3)$, $(1,0,n_3)$, $(1,1,n_3)$, $(2,0,n_3)$, $(2,1,n_3)$
--- were measured against the volume reference at both frequencies: over those forty rows
the worst per-entry error is $1.0\times10^{-14}$ and the worst per-part
$1.17\times10^{-14}$, with the $k$-series side at $2.4\times10^{-17}$ to
$9.3\times10^{-17}$ and the octant side at $1.6\times10^{-15}$ to $1.0\times10^{-14}$.
(That sweep was run on the audited version at $\mathrm{tol} = 10^{-13}$; the shipped
selector moves each of those boundaries by at most one lattice step, and the offsets on
either side of the new position are covered by the rows above.) \emph{The accuracy is
continuous across every boundary; the cost is not} (Section~\ref{sec:def}, defect D3).

\subsection{Aspect ratios, and the parameter that governs everything}
\label{sec:ver:asp}

The requirement is aspect ratios $10^{-3}$ to $10^{3}$. Twelve shapes were built for this,
with aspect ratios $1$, $4$, $11.4$, $16$ (in all three axis permutations), $87.7$, $295$,
$678$ and $1020$, six shape-adapted offsets each --- two and four cells along the
\emph{shortest} edge, two along the longest, $(3,3,3)$, $(7,-3,2)$, $(16,16,16)$ --- and
four frequencies, giving $288$ tensors against the independent volume reference.

\textbf{The whole sweep collapses onto one parameter, $|k|r_{\mathrm{d}}$ with
$r_{\mathrm{d}} = \sqrt{s_1^2+s_2^2+s_3^2}$.} Not the longest edge: a needle whose long edge
is $0.503\lambda$ passes, and a cell whose long edge is $0.459\lambda$ fails at one
frequency and passes at another, because $|k|r_{\mathrm{d}}$ is $3.17$ in the first case and
$5.80$ in the second. On the audited version the break was at $|k|r_{\mathrm{d}} \approx 3.2$
and the error above it grew as $(|k|r_{\mathrm{d}})^{25.6}$, the exponent
$2n_{\max}+2 = 26$ of the frozen $k$-series (defect D9). With the adaptive $N$ of
Remark~\ref{rem:bnd:adapt} the four failing rows become

\noindent
i.e.\ a gain of $6.3\times10^{3}$ to $5.6\times10^{10}$, and every row now at or below
$1.71\times10^{-13}$ per entry with $7.3\times10^{-15}$ in max-norm. The two rows that miss
$10^{-13}$ per entry are aspect-$87.7$ and aspect-$678$ cells with longest edges of $0.92$
and $1.43$ wavelengths --- three to six times outside Gila's stated range --- and what is
left there is the small-part floor, visible in their imaginary-part errors of
$9.3\times10^{-12}$ on parts carrying less than $10^{-3}$ of their entry, not the
$k$-series. Inside Gila's range every shape has $|k|r_{\mathrm{d}} \le 2.72$ and passes with
margin.

\paragraph{The two finest cells, which failed for the opposite reason.} A $\lambda/128$
cube --- the finest cell Gila claims --- was wrong by a flat $4.65\times10^{-13}$ per entry
at \emph{every} separation from three to $128$ cells and at every $k\mathbf{R}$ from $0.255$
to $5.44$, because the per-shell $n$-cut was being chosen against a budget evaluated at a
radius where the expansion does not converge, and cut the $j_l$ series at two to four of its
thirteen stored terms. With the cut re-evaluated at the true radius the same sixteen rows run
from $2.6\times10^{-16}$ to $4.3\times10^{-15}$, a factor $110$ to $1800$, and the
separation-independence that diagnosed the bug is gone. The aspect-$4$ shape
$(1/16,1/32,1/64)$ went from $1.01\times10^{-12}$ at $(128,0,0)$ to $2.96\times10^{-15}$.
A third, independent mechanism --- the order $L$ under-selected at very large separation ---
was fixed by the same tolerance change: $(48,48,48)$ and $(64,64,64)$ on an aspect-$11.4$
shape went from $1.23\times10^{-13}$ and $1.19\times10^{-13}$ to $3.22\times10^{-15}$ and
$3.83\times10^{-15}$, with \emph{two} extra shells rather than the twelve the diagnosis
needed, because Theorem~A bounds the sum the code actually performs.

\noindent
\textbf{The three zeros prove bookkeeping, not accuracy, and should be read that way.}
$\mathbf{D}\to-\mathbf{D}$, the transpose and the axis reflections are exact \emph{by
construction} in this expansion: the whole-box table keeps only even $l$ and
$Y_{lm}(-\mathbf{u}) = (-1)^lY_{lm}(\mathbf{u})$, so route (i) cannot fail them. The honest
test is the axis permutation, which mixes different $(l,m)$ columns of the table and comes
out at $1.6\times10^{-15}$; it is not available on the slender cell, whose three axes are
inequivalent. Homogeneity, $T(\lambda f, \mathbf{s}/\lambda) = \lambda^{-2}T(f,\mathbf{s})$
at $\lambda = 3.7$, is $\le 6.6\times10^{-15}$. On route (ii), which reflects sub-boxes and
keeps odd $l$, the reflection test is \emph{not} vacuous and gives $1.6$ to
$2.7\times10^{-16}$, with the reflected offsets matching their own independent references to
$3.9\times10^{-16}$--$1.6\times10^{-15}$.

Number type: \code{ComplexF32} in gives \code{ComplexF32} out at $6.20\times10^{-8}$ of the
\code{Float64} value ($\varepsilon(\code{Float32}) = 1.19\times10^{-7}$), and
\code{Complex\{BigFloat\}} in gives \code{Complex\{BigFloat\}} out at $5.14\times10^{-16}$.
Changing the working precision from $128$ to $256$ bits moves the answer in the $38$th
digit, not the $16$th, so no untyped constant survives in the generic path. The ceiling is
$192$ bits, the precision at which the geometry table is stored
(Section~\ref{sec:def}, D5).

Gila's.

\subsection{The overlap: two independent expansions compared where both converge}
\label{sec:ver:ovl}

takes gives $5.3\times10^{-16}$ to $2.5\times10^{-15}$ and the forced $k$-series
$2.5\times10^{-17}$ to $6.7\times10^{-17}$, so the two routes agree with each other and with
the reference across the seam.
