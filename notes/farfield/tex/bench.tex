\section{The build, before and after}
\label{sec:bch}

\code{notes/farfield/bench.jl} drives the real \code{GlaVacOprMem} build stage by stage
through \code{GilaElectromagnetics.GilaVacuum}, on a quiet machine, and nothing under
\code{src/} is touched.

\emph{Before} is the stages of \code{genEgoCrcSlf!} called one by one: \code{wekTrp} (the
contact integrals, \code{intOrd = 48}), the threaded \code{egoFunInn!} loop over every
Toeplitz offset, \code{egoFunSng!} on the eight contact offsets, the identity term,
\code{egoToeCrc!} into the doubled circulant, \code{gthEgoCmp!}, \code{genEgoFur}. Since
\code{wekTrp} is memoized on (scale, order, frequency) it is timed once per shape and that
time is carried into both totals. \emph{After} is the same with
\code{farBlock!(egoToe, s, f)} in place of the \code{egoFunInn!} loop, \code{farSetup} timed
separately as the one-time table, everything else identical. Both end in
\code{GlaVacOprMem(cmpInf, egoFur, vol, vol)}, so both are usable operators, and
\code{bench.jl} checks that the staged \emph{before} path reproduces
\code{GlaVacOprMem}'s own \code{egoFur} \textbf{bit for bit} ($0.000\times10^{0}$ at every
size) before comparing anything.

\subsection{Where the time goes now}
\label{sec:bch:attr}

\textbf{The build is now the contact integrals and nothing else.} At $128^3$, $\lambda/32$
on twelve threads, $5.14$~s of the $6.73$~s total is \code{wekTrp} at \code{intOrd = 48};
the far field is $0.200$~s, the geometry-table setup $0.73$~s (and zero on the second
frequency of the same shape), the touching shell $0.060$~s, and the embedding, gather and
FFT together $0.60$~s. Single-threaded the same build is $28.59 + 0.70 + 1.49 + 0.05 +
3.43$~s. The \emph{after} total is nearly flat in $N$ --- $29.37$, $29.72$, $34.26$~s
single-threaded and $5.92$, $6.03$, $6.73$~s on twelve threads at $32^3$, $64^3$, $128^3$
--- because everything that grows with $N$ has been removed except the FFT, which is
$2.91$~s of the $128^3$ single-threaded total.

This is the attribution the task statement asked for, and it points at the next task rather
than at this one. \code{wekTrp} evaluates \code{wekS}, \code{wekE} and \code{wekV} by an
order-$48$ tensor rule; the companion document \emph{The Moments of Gila} gives closed forms
for every one of those integrals and a $k$-series built from them that reproduces the
order-$48$ values to $6\times10^{-11}$ and is about seven times faster. Section~\ref{sec:ver:pos}
adds the accuracy argument: on a slender cell that same order-$9$ shell rule is wrong by
$117\,\%$ and is the sole cause of the operator's indefiniteness. \emph{The contact
integrals are now both the whole cost and the whole error of the build, and the tool that
fixes them already exists.}

\textbf{That difference is Gila's error, not this library's.} The worst separated offset of
the $128^3$ block at $\lambda/32$ is $5.579\times10^{-13}$ at $(0,5,5)$, and
Section~\ref{sec:ver:ref} puts \code{farTensor} at $4.2\times10^{-15}$ against the $220$-bit
reference there; at $\lambda/8$ the worst is $1.882\times10^{-13}$ at $(0,2,0)$ and at
$\lambda/4$ it is $8.736\times10^{-14}$ at $(0,0,2)$. The \code{egoFur} difference
\emph{shrinks} as $N$ grows --- $5.3\times10^{-14}$ at $4^3$, $9.9\times10^{-15}$ at $128^3$
--- because the transform averages a fixed per-offset error over more offsets while
$\max|\code{egoFur}|$ is set by the self term. So the honest statement is:

\begin{quote}
\emph{The assembled operator changes by $6\times10^{-15}$ to $5\times10^{-14}$ relative,
and every part of that change is Gila's fixed Gauss--Legendre rule being replaced by
something $30$ to $10^{5}$ times more accurate.}
\end{quote}

\noindent
so $t(L) \approx 1.6$~ns per term above $L = 12$, and the whole-box evaluation costs under
a microsecond at every $L$ the far plateau uses. Route (ii), the eight octants, costs
$53$--$84~\mu$s at the offsets that need it ($67\,256$ to $104\,384$ terms at
$0.79$--$0.81$~ns each) --- and it is used at twelve offsets of two million.

The mean over a whole $32^3$ octant is $360$ terms per offset at $\lambda/32$, $473$ at
$\lambda/8$, $687$ at $\lambda/4$ and $644$ on the slender cell (the last including its
$67$ $k$-series and $104$ octant offsets), against the task statement's target of ``a few
hundred floating-point operations per offset''. \emph{The target is met in the currency of
terms and exceeded by a factor of a few in flops}, and the resulting fill is $706$ to
$1410$~ns per offset single-threaded ($0.185$~s for a $64^3$ block at $\lambda/32$,
$0.046$~s for a $32^3$ block at $\lambda/4$, $0.040$~s for a $32^3$ slender block including
its $67$ $k$-series offsets read from the cache) --- against $498.1~\mu$s for the order-$4$
Gauss--Legendre rule it replaces, a factor of $350$ to $700$ per offset before threading.
The $128^3$ fill of Section~\ref{sec:bch:time} is $1.491$~s over $2\,097\,144$ offsets,
i.e.\ $711$~ns each.
