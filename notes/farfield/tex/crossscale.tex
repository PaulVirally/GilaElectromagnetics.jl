\section{Unequal cells: the far field of a composite volume}
\label{sec:xs}

Everything before this section assumed that target and source cell have the same edges.
Gila's composite volumes (\code{GlaCmpVol}) put a coarse region next to a fine one, and the
external block between two regions of different scale is a family of pairs with
$\mathbf{s}_{\mathrm{t}} \ne \mathbf{s}_{\mathrm{s}}$. Touching unequal pairs are already
handled by the gcd-cell averaging of \code{egoCntOut!}; this section is about the
\emph{separated} ones. The mathematics changes in exactly one place, the per-axis weight,
and the rest of the method carries over with its proofs. What does not carry over is the
near band: the sub-box split the task statement proposed was implemented, measured, and
found not to converge where it was needed, and the near band is served instead by an
identity that reduces every unequal pair on Gila's lattice to a weighted box sum over the
equal-cell tensors of the fine cell. The section names every number's source; the five
sub-agent reports are \code{scratch/reports/xtheory.md}, \code{xtable.md}, \code{xgeom.md},
\code{xref.md}, \code{xnear.md}, the integration report \code{xunify.md}, and the
verification report \code{xverify.md}.

\subsection{The trapezoid weight}
\label{sec:xs:w}

For a target cell of edges $\mathbf{s}_{\mathrm{t}}$ centred at $\mathbf{R}$ and a source
cell of edges $\mathbf{s}_{\mathrm{s}}$ at the origin, put per axis
\begin{equation}
a_i = \tfrac12|s_{\mathrm{t},i}-s_{\mathrm{s},i}|,\qquad
b_i = \tfrac12(s_{\mathrm{t},i}+s_{\mathrm{s},i}),\qquad
b_i-a_i = \min(s_{\mathrm{t},i},s_{\mathrm{s},i}),\qquad
b_i^2-a_i^2 = s_{\mathrm{t},i}s_{\mathrm{s},i}.
\label{eq:xs:ab}
\end{equation}
The convolution of the two intervals is a trapezoid rather than a triangle: flat at height
$b_i-a_i$ on $|t|\le a_i$, falling as $b_i-|t|$ on $a_i\le|t|\le b_i$, zero beyond, with
kinks at $\pm a_i$ and $\pm b_i$ and none at $0$. The volume form of
Theorem~\ref{thm:vol:main} is otherwise unchanged,
$T_{ab} = V_{\mathrm{t}}^{-1}\int_D w\,[(\partial_a\partial_b+\delta_{ab}k^2)g](\mathbf{R}+\boldsymbol\delta)\,\dd\boldsymbol\delta$
on $D = \prod[-b_i,b_i]$ with $V_{\mathrm{t}} = \prod s_{\mathrm{t},i}$ the \emph{target}
volume (\code{srfMat} still divides by $V_{\mathrm{t}}$). The one fact that does all the
work is that
\begin{equation}
w_i(t) = (b_i-|t|)_+ - (a_i-|t|)_+ ,
\label{eq:xs:diff}
\end{equation}
\emph{the trapezoid is the triangle of half-width $b_i$ minus the triangle of half-width
$a_i$}. Every linear functional of $w_i$ is therefore a difference of two triangle
functionals, and the three-term moment formula of the task statement collapses to
\begin{equation}
\mu_n(a,b) = \int_{-b}^{b}w\,t^n\,\dd t = \frac{2\,(b^{n+2}-a^{n+2})}{(n+1)(n+2)}
= \mu_n(b) - \mu_n(a)\qquad(n\ \text{even};\ 0\ \text{for odd}\ n),
\label{eq:xs:mu}
\end{equation}
with $\mu_n(s)$ the triangle moment \eqref{eq:vol:mu}. Four consequences follow at once.
The weight is even on every axis, so the parity argument of Section~\ref{sec:exp:parity}
holds verbatim: only even $l$ survive and the same four $(l,m)$ classes are stored. The
geometry table is the equal-cell table \code{tabWhl} with \code{wgtTrpX(a, b)} in place
of \code{wgtTrpT(s)} and nothing else changed; at $a = 0$ the subtraction of an exact zero
leaves the same \code{BigFloat} operations, which is why the equal-cell tables are
bit-identical (Section~\ref{sec:xs:ver}). The table is stored divided by $\prod b_i$, and
since the raw integral is even under $(\mathbf{R},\mathbf{s}_{\mathrm{t}},\mathbf{s}_{\mathrm{s}})
\to(-\mathbf{R},\mathbf{s}_{\mathrm{s}},\mathbf{s}_{\mathrm{t}})$,
\begin{equation}
G(\mathbf{R};\mathbf{s}_{\mathrm{t}},\mathbf{s}_{\mathrm{s}}) =
\frac{V_{\mathrm{s}}}{V_{\mathrm{t}}}\,G(-\mathbf{R};\mathbf{s}_{\mathrm{s}},\mathbf{s}_{\mathrm{t}}),
\label{eq:xs:swap}
\end{equation}
so one table serves the \emph{unordered} pair, keyed on $(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})$; the
caller supplies the factor $\prod b_i/V_{\mathrm{t}}$ (folded into the frequency
prefactor) and the sign of $\mathbf{R}$. Finally $V_{\mathrm{t}}^{-1}\int_Dw =
\prod(b_i^2-a_i^2)/V_{\mathrm{t}} = V_{\mathrm{s}}$, so the a priori scale
$\mathrm{est}(\mathbf{R})$ of Section~\ref{sec:bnd2:meas}, which for equal cells
multiplied the pointwise dyadic scale by $\int w/V_{\mathrm{t}} = V_{\mathrm{t}}$, must
multiply by the \emph{source} volume $V_{\mathrm{s}}$; consistently,
$\mathrm{est}(\mathbf{R};\mathbf{s}_{\mathrm{t}},\mathbf{s}_{\mathrm{s}})/
\mathrm{est}(-\mathbf{R};\mathbf{s}_{\mathrm{s}},\mathbf{s}_{\mathrm{t}}) = V_{\mathrm{s}}/V_{\mathrm{t}}$
as in \eqref{eq:xs:swap}. The difference form \eqref{eq:xs:mu} loses at most
$\log_{10}(1/(1-(a/b)^{n+2})) \le 0.66$ digits in \code{Float64} at ratio $16$ and is
exact in the rational arithmetic the table is built in.\footnote{\code{scratch/reports/xtheory.md},
Section~1.1; the ratio-$16$ figure is $a/b = 15/17$ at $n = 0$.}

\subsection{The bounds: Theorem A$'$, and why Theorem B needs no change}
\label{sec:xs:bnd}

For the bounds the two triangles are \emph{not} subtracted, because Theorem~A works with
positive measures. Write $w_i' = -\operatorname{sign}(t)\,\mathbf 1_{a_i<|t|<b_i}$ and
$w_i'' = \delta_{-b_i}+\delta_{b_i}-\delta_{-a_i}-\delta_{a_i}$ distributionally; the
positive measures are $|w_i'|\,\dd t$, the flat measure on the two ramps only, with moments
$\lambda_n(a,b) = 2(b^{n+1}-a^{n+1})/(n+1)$, and $|w_i''|$, four unit point masses with
moments $2b_i^{2i}+2a_i^{2i}$. At $a_i = 0$ the two masses at $\pm a_i$ merge into the
triangle's $2\delta_0$, whose moment vector $[2,0,0,\dots]$ is the equal-cell code's
\code{zro}. The analyticity radius is $r_{\mathrm{d}} = |\mathbf{b}| = \max_{\overline D}|\boldsymbol\delta|$
and $\rho = |\mathbf{b}|/|\mathbf{R}|$.

\begin{lemma}[integration by parts onto the trapezoid weight]
\label{lem:xs:ibp}
Extend $w$ by zero. For $\phi\in C^2$ near $\overline{D}$, $a\ne b$, $c$ the third axis, and $Q_a$
the rectangle in the axes $b,c$,
\begin{align}
\int_D w\,\partial_a\partial_b\phi &= \int_D\operatorname{sign}(\delta_a)\operatorname{sign}(\delta_b)\,
\mathbf{1}_{a_a<|\delta_a|<b_a}\mathbf{1}_{a_b<|\delta_b|<b_b}\,w_c(\delta_c)\,\phi\,\dd\boldsymbol{\delta},
\label{eq:xs:ibpo}\\
\int_D w\,\partial_a^2\phi &= \int_{Q_a}w_bw_c\Bigl[\phi\big|_{\delta_a=b_a}+\phi\big|_{\delta_a=-b_a}
-\phi\big|_{\delta_a=a_a}-\phi\big|_{\delta_a=-a_a}\Bigr]\dd\delta_b\,\dd\delta_c .
\label{eq:xs:ibpd}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Verbatim Lemma~\ref{lem:bnd2:ibp}: $w$ is Lipschitz and vanishes on $\partial D$, so both
boundary terms vanish; $w_a'w_b'$ is $\operatorname{sign}\operatorname{sign}$ on the ramps
and $0$ on a plateau, which is \eqref{eq:xs:ibpo}; and
$-\int w_a'\partial_a\phi = -\int_{-b_a}^{-a_a}\partial_a\phi+\int_{a_a}^{b_a}\partial_a\phi
= \langle w_a'',\phi\rangle$, which is \eqref{eq:xs:ibpd}. Only the support of $w_a'$ and the
location of the point masses changed. When $a_a = 0$ the last two evaluations coincide and
give $-2\phi(0)$: the triangle lemma.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Theorem~A for the trapezoid box]
\label{thm:xs:reg}
Let $\rho = |\mathbf{b}|/|\mathbf{R}|<1$. Then \eqref{eq:bnd2:reg} holds with
$r_{\mathrm{d}} = |\mathbf{b}|$, $V_{\mathrm{t}}$ the target volume in the prefactor, and,
for $l = 2p$,
\begin{align}
V^{ab}_l &= \sum_{i+j+q=p}\tfrac{p!}{i!j!q!}\,\lambda_{2i}(a_a,b_a)\,\lambda_{2j}(a_b,b_b)\,\mu_{2q}(a_c,b_c)
\qquad (a\ne b),\label{eq:xs:Vo}\\
V^{aa}_l &= 2\sum_{i+j+q=p}\tfrac{p!}{i!j!q!}\,\bigl(b_a^{2i}+a_a^{2i}\bigr)\,\mu_{2j}(a_b,b_b)\,\mu_{2q}(a_c,b_c)
+ |k|^2W_l,\qquad W_l = \sum_{i+j+q=p}\tfrac{p!}{i!j!q!}\,\mu_{2i}\mu_{2j}\mu_{2q}.
\label{eq:xs:Vd}
\end{align}
Every term is positive; only even $l$ contribute.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each step of the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:bnd2:reg} was checked against the new weight.
Steps that do not see $w$ are unchanged: $\partial_{\mathbf{R}} = \partial_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}$
on $g(\mathbf{R}+\boldsymbol{\delta})$, the representation
$T_{ab} = \frac{\ii k}{f^2V_{\mathrm{t}}}\sum(-1)^l\psi_{lm}(\mathbf{R})\mathcal{M}^{ab}_{lm}$
(it is the definition of the volume form with the new $w$), the Cauchy--Schwarz over $m$
and Lemma~\ref{lem:bnd2:msum}. Three steps see $w$. (i) Uniform convergence of
\eqref{eq:exp:g} with all $\boldsymbol{\delta}$-derivatives on $\overline D$ needs
$\max_{\overline D}|\boldsymbol{\delta}| = |\mathbf{b}| < |\mathbf{R}|$. (ii)
Lemma~\ref{lem:xs:ibp} replaces Lemma~\ref{lem:bnd2:ibp}, and Minkowski's integral
inequality applies because $|w_a'|\,\dd t$, $|w_a''|$ and $w\,\dd t$ are positive measures;
this is the only place $w\ge0$ is used. (iii) Theorem~\ref{thm:bnd:j} is applied with
$x\le r_{\mathrm{d}} = |\mathbf{b}|$, which holds on the ramps and at $\pm a_i$, $\pm b_i$,
all inside $\overline D$, and the multinomial expansion of $|\boldsymbol{\delta}|^{2p}$
against the product measures gives \eqref{eq:xs:Vo}--\eqref{eq:xs:Vd} with the new
one-dimensional moments. Odd $l$ vanish because the parity argument of
Section~\ref{sec:exp:parity} uses only that $w$ is even in every coordinate. Nothing else
changes; at $a_i = 0$ on every axis the statement is Theorem~\ref{thm:bnd2:reg} term by
term, and the code's \code{farMomW(lTop, a, b)} at $\mathbf{a} = 0$ reproduces
\code{farMomW(lTop, s)} bitwise in \code{Float64}, \code{BigFloat} and
\code{Rational\{BigInt\}} ($359$ comparisons, $0$ mismatches).\footnote{\code{scratch/xwork/xtheory/out\_xbit.txt}.}
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[sub-boxes and the gcd average]
\label{rem:xs:sub}
Theorem~\ref{thm:bnd2:sub} needs no change. Its hypotheses are an affine weight
$u_i = \alpha_i+\beta_it\ge0$ on $[-h_i,h_i]$ and $\rho_j = |\mathbf{h}|/|\mathbf{R}+\mathbf{c}_j|<1$.
A ramp $[a_i,b_i]$ is the octant piece with $h_i = (b_i-a_i)/2$, $\alpha_i = h_i$,
$\beta_i = \mp1$; a plateau $[-a_i,a_i]$ has $h_i = a_i$, $\alpha_i = b_i-a_i$, $\beta_i = 0$,
for which the one-dimensional identities of the proof hold with their $\beta$ terms absent
($\nu_i = \pi_i = (b_i-a_i)(\delta_{h_i}+\delta_{-h_i})$, no $\beta_i$-measure); the proof
never uses $\beta_i\ne0$ or $\alpha_i = h_i$. The gcd average
\begin{equation}
G(\mathbf{R};\mathbf{s}_{\mathrm{t}},\mathbf{s}_{\mathrm{s}}) = \frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{t}}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\mathrm{t}}}\sum_{j'=1}^{N_{\mathrm{s}}}
G(\mathbf{R}+\mathbf{c}_j-\mathbf{c}'_{j'};\mathbf{g},\mathbf{g}),\qquad g_i = \min(s_{\mathrm{t},i},s_{\mathrm{s},i}),
\label{eq:xs:gcd}
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf{c}_j$, $\mathbf{c}'_{j'}$ the centres of the $N_{\mathrm{t}}$ target and
$N_{\mathrm{s}}$ source sub-cells of edges $\mathbf{g}$, is the decomposition of the measure
$w\,\dd\boldsymbol{\delta}$ into $N_{\mathrm{t}}N_{\mathrm{s}}$ translated triangle
measures (a sum of indicator convolutions), so each piece is Theorem~\ref{thm:bnd2:reg} at
the real offset $\mathbf{R}+\mathbf{c}_j-\mathbf{c}'_{j'}$ with $r_{\mathrm{d}} = |\mathbf{g}|$,
and the errors add with the weights $1/N_{\mathrm{t}}$: a budget of
$\mathrm{tol}\cdot\mathrm{est}/N_{\mathrm{s}}$ per piece certifies $\mathrm{tol}\cdot\mathrm{est}$
for the sum. The factor $1/N_{\mathrm{t}}$ and not $1/(N_{\mathrm{t}}N_{\mathrm{s}})$ is
because $G$ already carries the source volume.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[the scale, the lower bound, the $n$-tail]
\label{rem:xs:est}
The proven lower scale $\mathrm{estLo}$ keeps its two hypotheses with $r_{\mathrm{d}} = |\mathbf{b}|$:
$|k|\,|\mathbf{b}|\le\pi$, true for all twelve test shapes of Section~\ref{sec:xs:gila} at
$g = \lambda/32$, $f = 1$ ($0.405$ at ratio $2$ on one axis to $2.89$ at ratio $16$ on
three), and the $l\ge2$ tail below the leading shell, whose factor becomes
$|k|V_{\mathrm{s}}\,j_0(|k||\mathbf{b}|)/(4\pi|f|^2)$. Measured, the second fails
($\mathrm{estLo} = 0$) at the nearest offsets for ratio $\ge4$ on two or three axes and
at every offset up to $32$ fine cells for ratio $16$ on two or three axes; where it holds,
$\mathrm{estLo}/\mathrm{est}$ is $0.005$--$0.03$ at one cell of gap and $0.02$--$0.42$ at
thirty-two, so $\mathrm{est}$ remains the scale, as for equal cells.\footnote{\code{scratch/xwork/xtheory/out\_xlow.md}.}
The $n$-tail certificate of the $k$-series order integrates the same pointwise remainder
majorant against the same measures, so it is Theorem~\ref{thm:xs:reg} with
$|k|^le^{\cdots}/(2l+1)!!\to\mathrm{jtail}(l,N,|k||\mathbf{b}|)/|\mathbf{b}|^{l+2N+2}$ and
$V^{ab}_l\to V^{ab}_{l+2N+2}$; in the code \code{nCutVec} now takes the prefactor volume
$V_{\mathrm{t}}$, the scale volume $V_{\mathrm{s}}$ and $r_{\mathrm{d}}$ as separate
arguments. Route (iii)'s $x = |k|D_{\max}$ uses
$D_{\max} = \bigl(\sum_i(|R_i|+b_i)^2\bigr)^{1/2}$, the largest distance between a point of
the target and a point of the source, which is what \code{panSpan} already returns for
unequal panels.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Where Gila produces unequal separated pairs, and how it computes them today}
\label{sec:xs:gila}

Reading \code{src/glaCmpOpr.jl}, \code{glaCmpVol.jl} and
\code{vacuum/glaVacOprMemGen.jl}, and confirming each statement by logging the calls of a
real \code{GlaVacOprMem} build, gives a picture that differs from the task statement's in
one architectural respect.\footnote{\code{scratch/reports/xgeom.md}, Sections~0, 1, 1b, 5;
scripts \code{scratch/xwork/xgeom/enum.jl}, \code{instr.jl}, \code{nest.jl}.} A composite
region pair of different scale whose bounding boxes \emph{touch} (face, edge or corner; the
test is a closed-box test) is not built with unequal cells at all: \code{\_sndBlk} remeshes
the coarse region at the fine scale and builds an \emph{equal-cell} external operator with
the contact correction, wrapped in \code{GlaSndOprVac}. For a $64^3$ fine region against a
$4^3$ neighbour of $16g$ that is a $64^3\times64^3$ equal-cell block at $g$ with
$2.1\times10^{6}$ offsets, all of them the equal-cell library's. The unequal-cell
\code{genEgoCrcExt!} runs in two situations: (a) the direct constructor
\code{GlaOprVac(trg, src)} on two \code{GlaVol}s of different scale, touching or not, which
is the route the test suite exercises (\code{test/crsSclTest.jl}); and (b) composite region
pairs of different scale that do \emph{not} touch, i.e.\ nested refinement (a fine core
inside a ring inside a coarse shell, core against shell) and two-body operators. In (b) the
ring cannot be one coarse cell thick: \code{chkCmpVol} demands even cell counts per region
and \code{chkParCmpVol} an even per-partition sum for every region pair, and a
one-coarse-cell ring of any finer scale gives an odd sum and is rejected, so the thinnest
legal gap is \emph{two} coarse cells.

\paragraph{The lattice.} Every non-contact offset $\mathbf{R}$ of both routes has
$2\mathbf{R}/g$ integer, and $R_i/g$ is a half-integer on every coarsened axis of even
ratio: $R_i = (r_i+1)g/2+\kappa g$ for a face gap of $\kappa$ fine cells, and the lateral
positions $(r_i-1)g/2 - jg$ are half-integers too. An unequal-cell table would therefore be
keyed on the half-lattice $2\mathbf{R}/g$, which is what the block's circulant index already
is. The gcd average \eqref{eq:xs:gcd} is where the half-integers cancel: the source
sub-cell centres $c'_j = (-(r-1)/2+j)g$ are half-integers for even $r$, so
$R_x - c'_j = (r+\kappa-j)g$ runs over the \emph{integer} fine offsets $\kappa+1,\dots,\kappa+r$,
and laterally $(r-1)/2-j'+\mathrm{lat}/g$ is an integer for the same reason. The nearest
sub-offset of the $\kappa = 1$ layer has max-norm $2$, the one-fine-cell-gap offset that the
equal-cell library routes to the octants. Since $g = 1/32$ and $r$ is a power of two, every
offset is a dyadic rational, the \code{Float64} values Gila's \code{grdSel} produces are
exact, and no classification depends on rounding.

\paragraph{Offset counts and classes.} Per cell pair Gila classifies: contact if
$|R_i| < \mathrm{cntTol}+(s_{\mathrm{t},i}+s_{\mathrm{s},i})/2$ on every axis
(\code{egoCntOut!}, gcd-cell self-tensor averaging); else adaptive
(\code{egoSrfAdp!}, \code{hcubature} at relative tolerance $10^{-6}$, absolute $10^{-9}$) if
$\mathrm{sep} = \max_i\operatorname{round}(|R_i|/\max(s_{\mathrm{t},i},s_{\mathrm{s},i}))\le1$;
else the fixed rule \code{egoSrfFxd!} with \code{quadOrd(sep, kScl)}. On the test-suite
geometry (a coarse $2^3$ block of cells $rg$ on the axes of the set $A$ sharing its full
face with a fine block of $g$, the fine cell the target) the exact-rational enumeration
reproduces the real build's logged $(\mathbf{R},\text{class},\mathrm{sep},\mathrm{ord})$
set with $0$ missing, $0$ extra and $0$ class disagreements on five builds, and obeys
closed forms: $27r^{|A|}$ distinct offsets, $\prod_{\text{lateral}}(r_i+2)$ contact
offsets, and the adaptive class is every offset with $|R_i|<1.5\max(s_{\mathrm{t},i},s_{\mathrm{s},i})$
on all axes minus the contact ones.\footnote{\code{scratch/xwork/xgeom/enum\_out.md},
\code{instr\_out.md}.}

\subsection{Three routes, and the one that was dropped}
\label{sec:xs:routes}

\paragraph{Route 1: the whole trapezoid box.} Wherever Theorem~\ref{thm:xs:reg} certifies
$\mathrm{tol}\cdot\mathrm{est}$ within the table ($L\le56$), one expansion per offset at
$0.9$--$12.5~\mu$s for $L = 12$--$56$, the same code as the equal-cell whole box with the
trapezoid table. On the realistic $\lambda/2$ block that is $1\,387\,696$ of the
$1\,404\,604$ non-contact offsets, $98.8\,\%$, and $4.15$~s single-threaded summed over the
$L$ histogram, with $L\le24$ for $872\,468$ of them.\footnote{\code{scratch/reports/xtable.md},
Section~4; \code{scratch/xwork/xtable/census.txt}, \code{costsum.out}.} The band edge sits
at $\rho_{\mathrm{whl}} = 0.58$--$0.60$; below it the whole box converges, above it
($16\,908$ offsets here) it does not within $L = 56$.

\paragraph{Route 2: the gcd average as a box sum.} On Gila's lattice every sub-offset of
\eqref{eq:xs:gcd} is an integer offset of the fine cell, so the average is a weighted sum of
entries of the fine equal-cell \code{egoToe} that the equal-cell library already fills:
\begin{equation}
G(\mathbf{R}) = \frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{t}}}\sum_{\mathbf{t}}\Bigl(\prod_i m_i(t_i)\Bigr)\,
\mathrm{ego}\bigl[\,|\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{t}|\,\bigr]\ \text{(reflected)},\qquad
m_i(t) = \#\{(j,j')\colon j-j' = t\},
\label{eq:xs:box}
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf{m} = \mathbf{R}/g + (\mathbf{n}_{\mathrm{s}}-\mathbf{n}_{\mathrm{t}})/2$ the
integer lattice coordinate, $\mathbf{n}_{\mathrm{t}}$, $\mathbf{n}_{\mathrm{s}}$ the
sub-cell counts per axis and
$\prod_i(n_{\mathrm{t},i}+n_{\mathrm{s},i}-1)$ weighted adds per offset, i.e.\ a
convolution of the fine block with the coarse cell's indicator, all weights $+1/N_{\mathrm{t}}$.
The cancellation of the sum, $\Lambda = \sum_{jj'}\max|G_{jj'}|/(N_{\mathrm{t}}\max|\sum G_{jj'}|)$,
was measured at $1.0$--$2.3$ over the whole test matrix at all three frequencies
($1.0$ at ratio $2$, $1.1$--$1.3$ at ratio $8$, $1.5$--$2.3$ at ratio $16$ on three
axes),\footnote{\code{scratch/xwork/xtable/estratio\_f1.txt}, \code{estratio\_f2.md},
\code{estratio\_f3.md}; \code{xtable.md}, Sections~2d and~3e.} so the average loses at most
one bit to interference at these cell sizes; the a priori fear that it would cancel at
$\lambda/4$--$\lambda/2$ coarse cells was wrong, and the disagreements that raised it are
Section~\ref{sec:xs:def}. The form matters more than the formula: evaluated naively as
$N_{\mathrm{t}}N_{\mathrm{s}}$ separate equal-cell tensors per offset ($4096$ at ratio $16$
on three axes) it costs $38.9$~ms per offset and $658$~s for the $16\,908$ offsets of the
near band, $160$ times the whole far field; as the box sum it is one equal-cell
\code{farBlock!} on the $33^3$ fine block the band needs ($0.95$~s, $35\,929$ offsets) plus
$28.6~\mu$s of adds per offset ($0.48$~s), identical to the naive sum to the last bit at the
offset checked.\footnote{\code{scratch/reports/xtable.md}, Section~4, and Section~4b:
box sum against the per-offset average on $340$ offsets, worst $9.3\times10^{-16}$,
summation order.} The equal-cell tolerance of the pieces is the pair's tolerance; the a
posteriori certificate of the sum is the $1/N_{\mathrm{t}}$-weighted sum of the pieces'
certificates.

\paragraph{Route 3: the $k$-series on unequal panels.} Where the pair is off the gcd lattice
(a lateral coordinate $0$ against an axis whose source cell is an even multiple of $g$ puts
$m_i$ at a half-integer; Gila never produces it, the reference matrix does), or where a
sub-offset would touch, the moment series of the companion document is applied to the
thirty-six face pairs of the two cells, which \code{pairMoments} takes as arbitrary
axis-aligned rectangles, at $128$ bits with the order $N$ from the $k$-series bound at
$\mathrm{tol}/\Lambda$. It is a correctness fallback and not a cost: $6$--$21$~s per offset
at $N = 13$--$44$, reached by $103$ of the $438$ reference records and by $0$ offsets of
either block (Section~\ref{sec:xs:ver}).

\paragraph{The twenty-seven-piece split: implemented, measured, dropped.} The task statement
expected the near band to be served as for equal cells, by sub-boxes: with kinks at $\pm a_i$
the minimal legal split per axis is three pieces, two ramps $[\pm a_i,\pm b_i]$ of affine
weight and a plateau $[-a_i,a_i]$ of constant weight, twenty-seven sub-boxes with the
reflection rule as before, covered by Theorem~\ref{thm:bnd2:sub} with $\beta = 0$ on a
plateau axis (Remark~\ref{rem:xs:sub}). The split was built in full: eight piece-type tables
per pair (plateau bits per axis), selection by Theorem~B with the budget
$\mathrm{tol}\cdot\mathrm{est}$ over the pieces, and the reflection rule with the plateau
piece its own mirror.\footnote{\code{scratch/xwork/xtable/farx.jl}: \code{tabPce},
\code{tnsPceX!}, \code{boundLpce}; \code{xtable.md}, Sections~1, 1b, 2.} It converges

equal cells, with the thresholds of Section~\ref{sec:join:rule} recomputed from the
trapezoid moments and $\mathrm{est}$ with $V_{\mathrm{s}}$.

\subsection{Three defects found on the way}
\label{sec:xs:def}

\paragraph{D11: the $n$-cut masked its own failure.} The first whole-box results at the
$\lambda/4$ and $\lambda/2$ coarse cells disagreed with the gcd average and with the
reference by $5.9\times10^{-14}$ to $6.8\times10^{-10}$ while the gcd average sat at
$4\times10^{-16}$ to $2\times10^{-15}$ from the reference. Two causes, in one pair of lines
that the equal-cell \code{farSetup} shares.\footnote{\code{scratch/reports/xtable.md},
Sections~2d and~2e; \code{scratch/xwork/xtable/attrib.txt}, \code{ncutfull.txt}.} First,
the per-shell $k$-series order $N_l$ is certified at two radii, the nearest whole-box radius
and the farthest, and \code{nCutVec} returns $-1$ for ``no $N\le n_{\max}$ meets the
budget''; the two vectors were combined with \code{max.}, which turns an uncertified $-1$
at one radius into the other radius' value. Second, with the split enabled the near radius
was taken from the smallest \emph{piece} centre ($1.66g$ for a ramp piece at $\kappa = 1$),
where the whole-box $n$-tail with $r_{\mathrm{d}} = |\mathbf{b}|$ is vacuous ($\rho = 8$)
and returns $-1$ at every $l\gtrsim10$; the far-end value, $N = 0$ at high $l$ where the
far radius is $3300$--$7500g$, was then used at the near offsets of $11$--$27g$, whose
high-$l$ columns need $N = 1$--$4$, and \code{frqBox} contracted those columns with the
$n = 0$ term alone. The fix is \code{cutMax} (elementwise maximum with $-1$ absorbing, so
an uncertified slot propagates and triggers the recomputation at $N_{\mathrm{cap}}$) and the
whole-box $n$-cut certified at whole-box radii only. The four disputed offsets before and
after, $f = 1$, against the $220$-bit reference:

\paragraph{D12: the table cache was not multi-process safe.} Two Julia processes
extending the same shape-table file each appended the missing $l$-segment (the append lock
is per process and nothing re-reads before appending), and a later reader concatenated the
duplicated $(l,m)$ columns and summed the shells $l = 40$--$54$ twice: $4.68\times10^{-13}$
at $(6.5,1.5,1.5)g$ on the ratio-$4$ cube at $L = 54$ against a certificate of
$3.4\times10^{-14}$, a violation by $14\times$, where the same offset from a clean table
is $5.12\times10^{-16}$.\footnote{\code{scratch/reports/xunify.md}, Section~D.0;
\code{scratch/xwork/xunify/attrib*.jl}; the corrupted file is kept in
\code{xwork/xunify/tab\_corrupt/}.} The file held $602$ columns of which $406$ were
distinct. The fix (\code{mrgGeo}) drops a segment's columns whose $(l,m)$ index is already
present when merging, so a duplicated file reads correctly ($406$ columns,
$5.12\times10^{-16}$) and a well-formed file reads exactly as before (bit-identity
re-proved below). The writer still appends without a cross-process lock; a duplicated
segment now costs only disk ($2.80$ against $1.88$~MB for one pair table).

\paragraph{D13: a frequency set reused at a radius where its $n$-cut was never certified.}
Found by the adversarial agent of Section~\ref{sec:xs:adv} and the one defect it had to
fix.\footnote{\code{scratch/reports/xverify.md}, Sections~5 and~2b~(8);
\code{scratch/xwork/xverify/k\_adv\_prefix.txt} is the pre-fix table, \code{farfield\_prefix.jl}
the pre-fix source.} \code{farSetupX} certifies the $k$-series cut of a pair's whole-box
table at two radii: the far end $r_{\mathrm{hi}} = 4n_{\mathrm{blk}}r_{\mathrm{d}}$ and the
near end $r_{\mathrm{nc}}$, the smallest $|\mathbf{R}|$ among the offsets it was given that
takes route 1. The $n$-tail relative to $\mathrm{est}$ decreases with $|\mathbf{R}|$ for
$l\ge2$, so the set is certified on $[r_{\mathrm{nc}}, r_{\mathrm{hi}}]$ and nowhere else,
and nothing checked the radius of a later request. Holding a \code{FrqSetX} across blocks is
exactly what the interface recommends, to skip the two- to three-second selection pass; a
held set asked for an offset \emph{nearer} than any it was built from silently received the
far end's $N$. A set built for the pair $g$ against $(4g,g,g)$ at $f = 1$ from the single
offset $(3.5,0,0)g$, which takes route 2, and then asked for $(6.5,0,0)g$, which takes
route 1 at $L = 36$, returned a tensor wrong by $1.59\times10^{-11}$ against both the
$220$-bit reference and a freshly built set, under a certificate of $1.7\times10^{-14}$ of
$\max|G|$: a violation by $10^{3}$. The same set at $(34.5,0,0)g$ was correct
($4.4\times10^{-16}$), and at $(102.5,0,0)g$ --- past $r_{\mathrm{hi}}$ altogether ---
correct in fact ($3.5\times10^{-16}$) and certified by nothing. The fix stores
$r_{\mathrm{nc}}$ and $r_{\mathrm{hi}}$ in the set, has \code{farRouteX} raise on a route-1
request outside the interval, and certifies from the first route-1 threshold when the given
offsets place no route-1 offset at all --- without that third line a set built only from
near offsets has a certified interval collapsed to the single point $r_{\mathrm{hi}}$ and
refuses everything. Afterwards the reused set reproduces the fresh one bitwise at
$(6.5,0,0)g$ ($1.21\times10^{-15}$ against the reference), and $(102.5,0,0)g$ is refused by
name. \code{farTensorX} and \code{farBlockX!} pass their own offsets when they build the
set, so the check cannot fire on the documented call paths, and it did not change a single
value in the block runs; it fires only for a hand-built or reused set, which is the case
that was wrong. \emph{An interval of validity that is computed and then not carried on the
object is not a certificate either.}

\subsection{Verification}
\label{sec:xs:ver}

\paragraph{The reference cache.}
\label{sec:xs:ref}
\code{scratch/xwork/xref/reftensors\_x.txt} holds $441$ records ($438$ distinct keys, three
written twice by two agents racing with identical values) of $220$-bit volume tensors for
unequal pairs, keyed on the exact rationals $(\mathbf{R},\mathbf{s}_{\mathrm{t}},\mathbf{s}_{\mathrm{s}},f)$:
the twelve coarse shapes (ratios $2,4,8,16$ on one, two and three axes against the fine
cell $g = 1/32$), face gaps $\kappa\in\{1,2,3,4,6,8,16,32\}$ along $x$ with the fine cell
centred, over a corner sub-cell and over the corner-corner sub-cell, a diagonal family past
the coarse edge at $\kappa\in\{1,2,4,8,16\}$, $f\in\{1,1+0.1\ii,0.37\}$, and two slender
pairs, $(1/32,1/32,1/512)$ against $(1/32,1/32,1/64)$ and against $(1/8,1/8,1/512)$, along
both their thin and their long axis; all $416$ keys of the planned matrix are
present.\footnote{\code{scratch/reports/xref.md}, Sections~3, 4, 6.} Two rules were
written. Rule~A is the face-pair reference of Section~\ref{sec:ver}, \code{pairKer} on the
thirty-six pairs of the two cells with the \code{srfSum!} signs, divided by
$V_{\mathrm{t}}$; its own order convergence (\code{ordN} $44$ against $64$) is
$1$--$7\times10^{-63}$ on all four cubic ratios, and it reproduces the equal-cell record at
$(5,1,0)$ to $0.0$. Rule~B is a graded tensor Gauss--Legendre volume rule with the trapezoid
weight, cut at $\pm a_i$ and $\pm b_i$ and graded by factors of two toward the singularity,
sharing no code with rule~A; it is $10$--$20$ times cheaper on these geometries
($16.5~\mu$s per point, $0.4$ to $20$ million points, $5$ to $330$~s per record) and is the
production rule of the cache, with rule~A the independent check. The two agree to
$1.6\times10^{-58}$--$3.9\times10^{-57}$ at a face gap of one fine cell and to
$1$--$2\times10^{-63}$ (the $220$-bit floor of a nine-entry sum is $1$--$4\times10^{-64}$)
from two cells on; the near-gap discrepancy is rule~B's order-$32$ truncation on the two
panels of width $g/2$ nearest the singularity (order $40$ brings it to $1.4\times10^{-64}$),
and its largest value, $6.8\times10^{-53}$ at ratio $16$ on three axes and $\kappa = 1$, is
the same $32$-point error on the same outermost panel in both rules. Every record of the
twelve coarse shapes is therefore good to at least $52$ digits and most to $58$--$63$; the
least accurate record of the cache is the slender pair's thin-axis $\kappa = 1$ record at
$3.0\times10^{-50}$, measured against both a higher order and rule~A. The gcd identity

\subsection{The near field of unequal cells (item 5)}
\label{sec:xs:near}

The task statement asked whether the thin-indicator regime of \code{moments.jl}, the
perpendicular box with one axis narrow relative to its offset ($2(\mathrm{hi}-\mathrm{lo})<\mathrm{hi}$)
that is integrated by a precision-adaptive Gauss--Legendre rule against the two-dimensional
closed forms, loses digits on the geometries unequal cells produce, and whether the
three-dimensional analogue of the centred Taylor series that removed the same problem in two
dimensions exists.\footnote{\code{scratch/reports/xnear.md}; scripts
\code{scratch/xwork/xnear/geom.jl}, \code{facedig.jl}, \code{box3tay.jl}, \code{boxval.jl},
\code{hybrid\_m.jl}, \code{ksr128.jl}, outputs in \code{xwork/xnear/out/}.} Both were
answered by measurement.

The branch is not an off-lattice exotic. A census of every three-dimensional box of every
face pair over the twelve coarse shapes, the equal-cell control, all touching and separated
positions up to thirty-two fine cells ($234$ position classes) shows that it fires on
\emph{every} separated pair, equal cells included: the fine-width indicator against the near
source face is $[\kappa,\kappa+1]$ in units of $g$, which sits exactly on the strict gate at
$\kappa = 1$ and is thin for $\kappa\ge2$, so the equal-cell lattice at $|\mathbf{D}| = 2$
escapes and $|\mathbf{D}|\ge3$ does not; on touching pairs it fires once a ratio is $\ge2$
(the far source face at $[r,r+1]$). The statement ``never reached on the lattice'' in the
companion document is about the fifteen touching sub-geometries of equal cells only. The
$k$-series route (iii) of Section~\ref{sec:join:slender} therefore already runs $99\,\%$ of
its perpendicular boxes at $|\mathbf{D}|\ge3$ through this branch. At $128$ bits, where
route (iii) runs, the branch loses $0$ to $0.99$ of $38.2$ digits against $320$ bits on the
seven face pairs measured ($5.8\times10^{-38}$ relative at worst), twenty-two orders below
route (iii)'s target; nothing in the $k$-series route changes.

In \code{Float64}, over $2772$ face pairs ($8$ shapes $\times$ $11$ positions $\times$
$36$ pairs) against the same formulas at $320$ bits, and confirmed identical on $504$ of
them against the independent graded quadrature of \code{notes/gen/ref/mom.jl}: the worst
loss is $0.99$ digits, on a \emph{parallel} pair through the two-dimensional integrator; the
worst thin-branch pair loses $0.93$ (two thin axes, $m = 0$); the worst closed-form
three-dimensional pair $0.90$ (the $m = -1$ seed of a corner box, identical for equal and
unequal cells); no pair loses a digit. Per box, over the $2228$ thin-branch boxes of a
$2734$-box sweep, the Gauss--Legendre rule loses $0.51$ / $0.71$ at the median and $0.95$ /
$0.96$ at worst with one / two thin axes, the same level as the divergence-identity closed
forms it bypasses ($1.15$ per box). The cross-scale geometries, ratios $2$--$16$, gaps
$1$--$32$ fine cells, add nothing to the loss of the equal-cell lattice ($0.91$ / $0.87$ in
the companion document).

The three-dimensional centred Taylor series exists and was written (\code{box3Tay},
generic in the number type): $r^m$ expanded about the box centre in the three shifted
coordinates with a three-index recurrence from $g\,\partial_pf = (m/2)f\,\partial_pg$, the
level sums against the exact affine moments, the same five controls as the two-dimensional
one, and a polydisc gate $\lambda>1/2$ with $\lambda$ the positive root of
$|\mathbf{h}|^2\lambda^2+2(\mathbf{u}_m\!\cdot\!\mathbf{h})\lambda-\rho^2 = 0$. It has a trap
the two-dimensional series cannot have: at $m = -1$ the function $1/r$ is harmonic, so on a
diagonal centre $\mathbf{u}_m = (a,a,a)$ every pure second derivative vanishes and level $2$
is exactly zero; the two-dimensional stopping rule of two consecutive small levels then stops
at $n = 3$ with the seed wrong by $3\times10^{-5}$ to $3\times10^{-3}$ ($11$--$13$ digits
lost on three boxes of the sweep). Three consecutive small levels and $n\ge4$ fix it. With
the fix the series converges on $94.6\,\%$ / $99.8\,\%$ of the one- / two-thin-axis boxes at
all fourteen $m$, is rejected on $1.0\,\%$ (all at $\lambda = 0.463$--$0.497$, a thin axis
beside a long corner axis on the ratio-$16$ shapes), and where it converges is at one
$\varepsilon$ (median $0.00$, worst $0.35$ digits, condition number $\le3.4$). It costs
$2$--$3$ times the Gauss--Legendre rule per call at the median, $6.3$ times in aggregate over
the sweep, and $2126$ times at worst ($115$ levels at $\lambda = 0.67$); gated at
$\lambda\ge1$ it caps the levels at $64$ for $1.9$ times the aggregate cost. At face-pair
level the worst case does not follow the box: $558$ of the $1595$ thin pairs also hold
closed-form corner boxes at $\lambda = 0.41$--$0.50$ where no series converges, pinning them
at $0.71$, and the closed-only pairs stay at $0.90$, so the global worst moves from $0.93$
to $0.90$ (series on thin boxes) or $0.71$ (series attempted on every box, at $2.4$ / $33$
times the median / $90$th-percentile cost). Less than one binary digit, for a second code
path, in a fill that does not run in \code{Float64}. \textbf{The recommendation, followed,
is to keep \code{box3Slv}; \code{moments.jl} is unchanged.} If a \code{Float64} near-field
fill is ever wired and its budget is counted in $\varepsilon$, the drop-in is
\code{scratch/xwork/xnear/box3tay.jl} gated at $\lambda\ge1$, with the three-level stopping
rule.

\paragraph{$\mathrm{est}$ is a scale, not a bound, in both directions.} Across the thin
axis of the slender pair $(1/32,1/32,1/512)$ against $(1/8,1/8,1/512)$ at $2$--$9$ thin
cells of separation the tensor is $10^{2}$ to $10^{4}$ below the pointwise scale, so
$\mathrm{est}/\max|G|$ is $100$, $550$, $2500$, $8100$ and the route-3 certificates there
are $1.5\times10^{-14}$ to $8.1\times10^{-13}$ of $\max|G|$ against actual errors of
$2.3\times10^{-17}$ to $9.0\times10^{-17}$: valid and loose by $2600$--$8900\times$. In the
other direction $\mathrm{est}$ \emph{under}-states $\max|G|$ by $2$--$4\times$ on the
ratio-$16$ rod at $\kappa = 1$, $f = 1$ and $0.37$ ($\mathrm{est}/\max|G| = 0.50$ and
$0.25$), so the a priori certificate there is $2$--$4$ times tighter than
$\mathrm{tol}$.\footnote{\code{scratch/reports/xunify.md}, Section~C, route-3 table;
\code{xtable.md}, Sections~3b and~3d.} The a posteriori certificate \eqref{eq:cert:post}
discharges both, as for equal cells.

\paragraph{$0.13\,\%$ of the realistic block is certified only to $1$--$4\,\mathrm{tol}$.}
$1800$ offsets at $\rho_{\mathrm{whl}} = 0.55$--$0.60$ meet the bound at $L = 56$ with
$1$--$4$ times $\mathrm{tol}\cdot\max|G|$ and the table has no more shells. Where a
reference exists they are correct to $3\times10^{-15}$ ($(24.5,7.5,7.5)g$ at $L = 54$:
$2.9\times10^{-16}$), but their proven bound is what it is. $L_{\max}>56$ or routing that
band to the box sum (the fine block would grow from $33^3$ to about $41^3$) would tighten
it.\footnote{\code{scratch/reports/xtable.md}, Section~4b; \code{xunify.md}, Section~D.2 and
open item~6.}

\paragraph{The table writer is unlocked.} D12 made a duplicated segment harmless on read;
the append itself still has no cross-process lock, so two processes extending one table file
still write the duplicate and pay its disk space.

\paragraph{The exact-rational offsets cost as much as the far field.} On the large block the
selection pass of \code{farSetupX} ($2.1~\mu$s per offset) and the route pass
($1.0~\mu$s) are $4.2$~s single-threaded against $4.4$~s of whole-box expansions, and on
twelve threads the serial selection ($2.9$~s) is half the total; the offset vector as
$\code{NTuple\{3,Rational\{BigInt\}\}}$ is about $1$~GB for $1.4\times10^{6}$ offsets, most
of the $2.95$~GB peak. A caller holding the \code{FrqSetX} skips the selection pass; an
integer-lattice interface ($\mathbf{m}$ and the gcd cell instead of $\mathbf{R}$) would
remove both passes, and is the natural interface for Gila's circulant, whose index already
is that lattice.

\paragraph{Route 3 costs $6$--$21$~s per offset} at $N = 13$--$44$ and $128$ bits on the
thirty-six unequal panels, is reached only off the gcd lattice, which Gila's block geometry
does not produce ($0$ of $1.4\times10^{6}$ offsets), and was verified only through the
$103$ off-lattice records of the reference matrix, never through a block. Its results are
cached to disk per (pair, orientation, frequency).

\paragraph{Other limits.} The fine equal-cell set of the near band costs $0.8$--$0.95$~s of
serial \code{farSetup} per \code{farBlockX!} call that starts without a \code{FrqSetX}
(the threaded fill of the $33^3$ block is $0.01$--$0.04$~s); \code{fineSet} caches it in
the pair set. The verification covers ratios $2,4,8,16$ on one to three axes against a
$\lambda/32$ fine cell and two slender pairs, at three frequencies; odd ratios, which
\code{GlaExtInf} allows and Gila's power-of-two refinements never use, put $\mathbf{R}$ on
the integer lattice and were exercised only by the exact-rational checks (ratio $3$). No
number in this section was measured on a GPU. And, as for equal cells, nothing under
\code{src/} was touched: \code{farBlockX!} fills a $3\times3\times n$ array for a list of
exact offsets, and wiring it into \code{genEgoCrcExt!} (whose \code{egoFunOut!} loop is
what it replaces, with \code{egoCntOut!} left in place for the contact class) is the next
task, together with the equal-cell wiring it shares a table directory with.
