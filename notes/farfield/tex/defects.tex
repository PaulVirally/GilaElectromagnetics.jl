\section{Defects found on the way}
\label{sec:def}

Two kinds: defects in the code being replaced or in the round-1 sources, and defects in this
work's own library, found by the adversarial audit and by the merge pass. Both are recorded
because both cost real accuracy and because the second list is what the reader should look
at first if a number here ever fails to reproduce.

\subsection{In Gila and in the round-1 sources}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
\item \textbf{\code{quadOrd} keys the rule order on the separation in cells, not on the
physical geometry.} On the slender cell $(1/32,1/32,1/512)$ the assembled tensor along the
short axis is wrong by $0.789$, $0.227$, $3.68\times10^{-2}$, $4.50\times10^{-3}$,
$1.79\times10^{-3}$, $2.97\times10^{-6}$ at $n = 2,3,4,6,8,16$ --- a $1/512$ boundary layer
across a $1/32$ face is invisible to a rule indexed by cell count
(Section~\ref{sec:grd:err}).
\item \textbf{The order-$9$ touching-shell and contact rule is wrong by $117\,\%$ on a
slender cell.} Measured against the exact moment series
(Section~\ref{sec:ver:pos}): $1.17$ relative at $(0,0,1)$, $0.127$ at the contact cell,
$0.024$--$0.037$ at the four singly-shifted offsets. It is the sole cause of the
indefiniteness of that block's anti-Hermitian part, which two passes of this work had
attributed to the discretization.
\item \textbf{The face-pair assembly amplifies.} \code{srfSum!} subtracts nearly equal
numbers; the amplification is $36$ to $1300$ at $\lambda/32$ and $4.3\times10^{3}$ to
$1.67\times10^{5}$ on the slender cell's long axes, turning per-pair errors of
$10^{-15}$ into tensor errors of $10^{-11}$. It is a \emph{fine}-cell effect, growing like
$(|\mathbf{R}|/s)^2$. The volume form removes it.
\item \textbf{A tail bound that is a partial sum.} One round-1 routine returned
$\mathrm{cum}[i] = \sum_{L<l\le l_{\mathrm{top}}}$, so $\mathrm{cum}[\mathrm{end}] = 0$ and
the bound certified $L = l_{\mathrm{top}}$ for free. Fixed by summing to $L + 16$ and
offering cuts only at $l \le L$; the discarded $l > L+16$ part is below $10^{-40}$ of the
retained tail at every shape and frequency used.
\item \textbf{A geometry table that accepted \code{Float64} edge lengths.} The contraction
cancels by $2.1\times10^{5}$ at $L = 20$, $5.8\times10^{8}$ at $L = 40$ and
$7.31\times10^{11}$ at $L = 56$: a \code{Float64} table is noise beyond $L \approx 26$. The
shipped code takes \code{Rational\{BigInt\}} only.
\item \textbf{A Bessel seed normalised on $\sin z/z$ recomputed from the \code{Float64}
argument.} At $\lambda/4$ and $128$ cells $k\mathbf{R}$ reaches $348$ and the rounding of
$z$ alone injects $7.7\times10^{-14}$ of phase --- at the target. Replaced by a
double-double phase, twelve flops.
\item \textbf{The $(-1)^l$ of the addition theorem.} It is invisible on the whole box, where
the triangle weight kills every odd $l$, and mandatory on a sub-box, where the affine weight
does not: omitting it is a $3.7\times10^{-2}$ error. The library folds it into the frequency
contraction for both tables, so the two paths cannot diverge.
\item \textbf{A per-thread workspace sized by \code{nthreads()} and indexed by
\code{threadid()}.} In Julia 1.12 the first counts the \code{:default} pool and the second is
a global id that also numbers the interactive threads; with \code{JULIA\_NUM\_THREADS=8}
this raises \code{BoundsError} at index 9. \code{maxthreadid()} is the correct size. Found by
the bit-identity check between the threaded and serial fills.
\item \textbf{\code{pairMoments} fails on \code{Rational\{BigInt\}} panels}
(\code{sqrt} at \code{moments.jl:442}). Convert to \code{BigFloat} first, which is exact for
these lengths.
\item \textbf{Gila's two separated-pair rules on unequal cells} (Section~\ref{sec:xs:gila}).
The adaptive rule \code{egoSrfAdp!} runs \code{hcubature} at relative tolerance $10^{-6}$
and delivers $2.7\times10^{-9}$ to $2.28\times10^{-4}$, median $1.2\times10^{-6}$, on
every offset of the first coarse cell of separation ($36\,540$ offsets of a $\lambda/2$
block) and not only in the touching layer. The fixed rule \code{egoSrfFxd!} is
$10^{-12}$ on cubes and slabs but $1.77\times10^{-4}$ on a $16{:}1{:}1$ rod approached
from the side, because \code{sep} is reached through the thin axis while the fine cell is
$1.5g$ from a $\lambda/2$ panel; and $1.7\times10^{-11}$ with order $6$ across a
$\lambda/2$ cell at $4\lambda$.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent
Four of these deserve a sentence, because the mechanism generalises.

\paragraph{D9 is the one that mattered, and it was invisible from inside Gila's range.} The
geometry table stores the first $N+1$ coefficients of the $k$-power series of
$j_l(k|\boldsymbol\delta|)$, and the order that series needs is set by $|k|r_{\mathrm{d}}$, a
shape \emph{and frequency} quantity. The code carried a constant $12$ that a per-shell cut
could only lower, so a shape needing more got $12$ and no warning. Every cell whose longest
edge is at most $\lambda/4$ has $|k|r_{\mathrm{d}} \le 2.72$ and needs $N \le 12$: the
constant was exactly right for the stated range, and the failure appeared only when the
audit deliberately went outside it. \emph{A constant that is correct on the whole tested
domain is not a certificate.} The fix is three lines and turns a silent
$9.6\times10^{-3}$ into a refusal.

\paragraph{D2 and D10 are one bug with two faces.} The same budget --- an absolute tolerance
against the a priori scale $\mathrm{est}$ --- was evaluated at a radius at which the
expansion does not converge, so the cut it returned was meaningless in both directions:
sometimes far too small (the $\lambda/128$ cube, wrong by $4.65\times10^{-13}$ at every
separation), sometimes saturated at the cap with no certificate (the extreme-aspect shapes,
wrong by up to $9.6\times10^{-3}$). Two symptoms that look like opposite bugs were one line.

\paragraph{D11 is D2 and D9 again, one layer up.} D2 evaluated the $n$-cut at a vacuous
radius; D9 capped $N$ at a constant that was right on the tested domain. D11 did both at
once and then hid the result: the near-radius evaluation was vacuous because it used a
sub-box centre with the whole-box $r_{\mathrm{d}}$, it returned the sentinel $-1$ as
designed, and \code{max.} replaced the sentinel by the far radius' $N$, which was $0$ at
high $l$ because the far radius is thousands of cells. The equal-cell library carried the
same two lines and was saved by its cells being at most $\lambda/4$, where both radii are
certified at $N\le6$. The first coarse cell outside that range ($\lambda/2$) exposed it
within a day of use. A sentinel that can be combined away by an arithmetic operator is not
a sentinel.

\paragraph{D4 is the cheapest lesson in the list.} \code{farRoute} computed a cost model on
every offset that all three of its call sites destructured as \code{\_}. It was $360$~ns of a
$936$~ns per-offset fill; making it a keyword recovered $28.5\,\%$ of the $32^3$ far-field
build and $31.3\,\%$ of the $64^3$ one, and changed no value.
